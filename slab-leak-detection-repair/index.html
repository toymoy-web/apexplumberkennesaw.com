A warm patch on the floor, a section of carpet that stays damp for no obvious reason, or a water bill that climbs with no change in usage are the three most common ways Kennesaw homeowners first notice a slab leak. Kennesaw Apex Plumbing traces the exact leak point electronically before cutting any concrete.

Why Slab Leaks Are So Common in Kennesaw

Kennesaw sits on Cobb County's red clay, a soil type that expands when wet and contracts when dry through the year. That constant movement puts steady pressure on the copper and PEX supply lines running under a slab foundation, and over ten to twenty years it is enough to wear a pinhole through a joint or a section of pipe.

Homes built before the late 1990s are more exposed to this because many still run the original copper lines from construction, while newer builds use PEX with fewer soldered joints. Either way, once a line under a slab starts leaking, the water has nowhere to go except up into the flooring or sideways under the foundation.

How Detection Works

A slab leak cannot be found by guessing where the sound is loudest. A technician isolates the water line, pressurizes it, and uses acoustic and electronic tracing equipment to follow the sound of the leak through the slab to within a few inches of the actual break. That precision is what keeps the eventual concrete cut small instead of tearing up an entire room.

Thermal imaging is used alongside acoustic tracing on hot water line leaks specifically, since the warm water creates a temperature difference at the surface that a camera can pick up even before any visible damage shows.

Repair Options

A single, clearly located leak is usually fixed with a direct spot repair, cutting a small section of slab and replacing the damaged pipe segment. If the home's copper lines are old enough that a second leak nearby is likely, rerouting the line through the attic or a wall cavity avoids cutting the slab again in a year or two, and a plumber should walk through both options and their real costs before starting either one.

What To Document For Insurance

  • Photograph any visible water staining, warped flooring, or damp carpet before repairs begin.
  • Keep the technician's written diagnosis, since it documents the leak was sudden rather than long-term neglect.
  • Save the water bill showing the usage spike, which insurers often ask for during a claim.

Slab leaks and water heater failures share the same red-clay root cause in a lot of Kennesaw homes, so a technician checking one will often flag early signs of the other. Homeowners dealing with a slab leak sometimes also need a sump pump if water has migrated toward a crawl space. The same red-clay soil pattern shows up in Acworth, just eight miles north.

Hot Water Line Leaks vs. Cold Water Line Leaks

A hot water line leak under a slab usually shows up faster because the warm water creates a noticeable temperature difference at the floor surface, which is why a warm patch is often the very first sign a homeowner notices. A cold water line leak is quieter and tends to announce itself through a rising water bill or a damp patch of carpet well before any temperature change is felt.

Both types get traced the same way electronically, but a hot line leak typically gets fixed faster once found, since the visible warm spot already narrows the search area before the equipment is even turned on.

What Happens If a Slab Leak Goes Unaddressed

Water escaping under a slab does not stay contained. It erodes the soil beneath the foundation, and over months that erosion can lead to settling cracks in the slab itself, well beyond the cost of the original pipe repair. A leak that seems minor today, judged only by a small warm spot, is rarely minor by the time it has been running for half a year.

Repair work on a buried water line falls under the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted with Georgia's state amendments, which sets minimum standards for pipe material, joint method, and burial depth. A spot repair or reroute gets done to that code, not just to whatever fixes today's leak fastest.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you find a slab leak without tearing up the whole floor?
Acoustic and electronic leak tracing equipment, along with thermal imaging on hot water lines, pinpoints the leak to within a few inches before any concrete is cut.
Is a slab leak an emergency?
Yes. Left alone, a slab leak keeps eroding the soil and pipe around it and can eventually undermine part of the foundation, so same-day diagnosis is recommended.
Will insurance cover slab leak repair?
Most policies cover the cost of accessing and repairing the pipe when the leak is sudden, but not water damage caused by a leak that went unnoticed for a long time.
