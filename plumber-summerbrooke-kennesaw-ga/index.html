Homes From The 1980s And 1990s: What Usually Comes Up

As general plumbing information, not a Summerbrooke survey: a house that is thirty to forty years old is past the typical life of its original water heater, several times over if the first one was replaced once already. It has also lived through a long run of faucet cartridges, toilet fill valves, supply hoses and angle stops. None of that means the house has a problem. It means those are the items worth checking first, and that replacing them on your schedule is cheaper than replacing them during a leak.

If a tank is leaking from the base or is badly rusted, it is replaced, not repaired. Our water heater installation and replacement page explains sizing and what the job involves, and water heater repair covers the cases where a repair still makes sense.

One Specific Thing To Check: Polybutylene

Polybutylene supply pipe, a grey or blue flexible plastic, was widely installed in American houses from roughly the late 1970s into the mid 1990s and has a record of failing at its fittings. Because Summerbrooke's reported build window overlaps that period, it is worth a look in your own house, but we are not saying Summerbrooke homes contain it. Many do not. A technician can check the line where it enters the house or at the water heater in a few minutes, and if you find it, our repiping page explains the options.

Slow Drains And Clogs

Mature landscaping and older drain lines can combine to cause recurring clogs. A drain that slows in one fixture is usually local, and one that slows in several fixtures at once points at the main line. When that happens, see our drain cleaning page, and our sewer line repair page if a camera shows damage. Do not keep using a backed-up main line, because the water has nowhere else to go.
