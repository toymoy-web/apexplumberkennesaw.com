How to Prepare Your Plumbing for a Georgia Winter Freeze

By the Kennesaw Apex team

Georgia's winters are mild most of the time, which is exactly why an occasional hard freeze catches so many Kennesaw homeowners unprepared. Pipes here aren't built with the same freeze protection standard as homes in consistently cold climates, which makes basic prep genuinely worthwhile before a cold snap hits.

How Often Georgia Actually Sees Hard Freezes

Metro Atlanta, including Kennesaw, typically sees somewhere between a handful and a dozen nights per winter that drop into genuine hard-freeze territory, though this varies meaningfully year to year. That relative rarity is exactly why the basic prep steps here get forgotten between events, and why a freeze that does happen tends to catch more homes unprepared than a similar event would in a climate where freezing is a nightly winter occurrence.

Why Southern Homes Are More Vulnerable, Not Less

Homes in consistently cold climates are built with supply lines routed through conditioned interior space and insulated against expected freezing temperatures every winter. Georgia homes, built for a climate where hard freezes are occasional rather than routine, often have exposed pipe sections, particularly in garages, crawl spaces, and exterior walls, that were never insulated with freeze protection in mind, since it wasn't considered a serious enough recurring risk.

A Real Example of How Fast This Can Happen

A single overnight low in the low-to-mid 20s Fahrenheit, combined with wind exposure against an exterior wall, can freeze an uninsulated pipe section in as little as six to eight hours. This is exactly why Georgia's occasional hard freezes, forecast a day or two in advance, are worth taking seriously with the prep steps below rather than assuming the mild regional climate provides more of a buffer than it actually does during those specific nights.

The Pipes Most At Risk

Any supply line running through an unheated garage, an exterior wall on the north or west side of the house, or an unconditioned crawl space is the most vulnerable, since these areas can drop well below the interior temperature of the home during a hard freeze. Outdoor spigots and any exposed line feeding an irrigation system are also at high risk if not properly shut off and drained before winter.

What To Do Before A Hard Freeze Is Forecast

  • Disconnect garden hoses from outdoor spigots and, if the spigot isn't a frost-proof model, shut off its interior valve and drain the line.
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks located on exterior walls to let warmer household air reach the pipes.
  • Let a faucet fed by an exposed or vulnerable line drip slightly during the coldest overnight hours, since moving water resists freezing better than still water.
  • Locate the main water shutoff valve now, before an emergency, so it's not a scramble to find during a frozen-pipe crisis.

Insulating Exposed Pipes Properly

Foam pipe insulation sleeves, available at any hardware store, are inexpensive and straightforward to install on exposed sections in a garage or crawl space, and they meaningfully reduce freeze risk during an occasional hard freeze even though they're not a substitute for pipes routed through conditioned space. For a known trouble spot that's frozen before, heat tape designed specifically for pipes adds active protection during the coldest nights, though it should be installed following the manufacturer's instructions exactly, since improper use is a fire risk.

What Actually Happens When A Pipe Freezes

Water expands as it freezes, and that expansion is what causes a pipe to crack or split, not the cold itself. The actual burst often doesn't happen until the ice starts to thaw and pressure builds up behind the blockage, which is why a burst sometimes shows up a day or two after the coldest temperatures have already passed, catching homeowners off guard when they think the danger has passed.

If A Pipe Has Already Frozen But Not Burst

A frozen but intact pipe shows up as reduced or significantly diminished water flow from one or more specific fixtures during cold weather, sometimes affecting an entire section of the house at once. Applying gentle heat, a hair dryer on low, warm towels, or a space heater positioned safely nearby, to the suspected frozen section can thaw it before it bursts, but never use an open flame, and shut off the main valve first as a precaution in case the pipe has already cracked internally without an obvious leak yet.

Why Slab Foundation Homes Have A Different Risk Profile

Homes on slab foundations, common across Kennesaw, generally have less exposed exterior pipe than homes with crawl spaces, since the main supply lines run under the heated slab itself rather than through an unconditioned space. The higher-risk areas in a slab home are usually the garage, where a water heater or exposed supply line sits in an unconditioned space, and any exterior hose bibs.

Frequently Asked Questions

How cold does it actually need to get for pipes to freeze in Georgia?
Sustained temperatures below freezing for several hours, typically overnight lows in the mid-20s Fahrenheit or lower, put exposed or poorly insulated pipes at real risk.
Does letting a faucet drip really help prevent freezing?
Yes, moving water in a pipe is significantly more resistant to freezing than still water, which is why a slow, steady drip on a vulnerable line is a genuinely effective preventive step.
Should I turn off my water heater during a hard freeze?
No, keeping the water heater running normally is fine; the concern is exposed supply lines, not the water heater itself, which is typically in a protected location.
