Slab Leaks

Signs of a Slab Leak Every Kennesaw Homeowner Should Know

By the Kennesaw Apex team

A slab leak is one of the trickier plumbing problems to catch early, since the pipe is hidden beneath a concrete foundation and the signs it produces are often subtle enough to dismiss as something else entirely.

A Warm Spot On The Floor

The single most distinctive sign of a slab leak is a warm patch on an otherwise normal-temperature floor, caused by a hot water line leaking beneath the slab. This is most noticeable on tile or hardwood flooring, where the temperature difference transfers more readily than through carpet. If a specific area of flooring consistently feels warmer than the rest of the house with no other explanation, that's worth investigating specifically.

A Section Of Carpet That Stays Damp

A cold water line leak under a slab doesn't produce the same warmth, but it can still cause a section of carpet or flooring to stay damp or develop a musty smell with no obvious external water source. This is easier to overlook than a warm spot, since there's no temperature cue, but persistent unexplained dampness in one specific area is a real signal worth checking, particularly if it recurs after cleaning or drying the area out.

Why Some Homes Never Show These Signs Until It's Severe

A home with thick carpet padding or dense subfloor material can absorb a surprising amount of moisture before any visible dampness or odor reaches the surface, effectively delaying the warning signs described here. This is part of why two homes with a similar leak can present very differently, one catching it within weeks through a damp spot, another not noticing until the leak has been running for months.

A Rising Water Bill With No Other Cause

As covered in more detail in our post on unexplained high water bills, a leak that isn't visible anywhere in the home is one of the most common reasons a bill climbs unexpectedly. If the standard checks, running toilets, irrigation systems, and visible fixture leaks, have all come back clean, a slab leak becomes a much more likely explanation.

Why Detection Precision Actually Matters

Before electronic tracing equipment became standard, finding a slab leak sometimes meant opening multiple sections of flooring based on educated guesses, an expensive and disruptive approach compared to today's standard. Modern acoustic and thermal tracing narrows a leak's location to within a few inches in most cases, which is the difference between cutting a small, targeted access hole and tearing up a much larger section of flooring on a hunch.

The Sound of Running Water

Some slab leaks are audible, particularly if the leak point is significant, producing a faint hissing or running water sound audible when the house is otherwise quiet and no fixtures are in use. This is a less common sign than the others, but worth noting if it happens, especially late at night when household background noise is at its lowest.

Cracks In Flooring Or Baseboards

A slab leak left unaddressed for a long period can eventually cause the concrete slab itself to shift slightly as the soil beneath it erodes from the escaping water, which can show up as new cracks in tile, a baseboard separating slightly from the wall, or a door that starts sticking where it didn't before. These are later-stage signs, indicating the leak has likely been active and undetected for a considerably longer period already, often many months.

Why Kennesaw Sees This Pattern Often

Kennesaw's red clay soil shifts with moisture changes throughout the year, putting steady pressure on buried supply lines under slab foundations, which is common across the city's housing stock. Homes with original 1980s-1990s copper supply lines are at the highest risk, since decades of that soil movement have had the most cumulative effect on those older joints.

Hot Water Line vs. Cold Water Line Leaks

A hot water line leak under a slab tends to announce itself faster, since the warm water creates a detectable temperature difference at the floor surface fairly quickly. A cold water line leak is quieter, often showing up first through a rising water bill or damp carpet rather than any temperature cue, which means it can run for a longer period before anyone notices. Both are traced the same way electronically, but a hot line leak is typically caught and fixed sooner simply because it announces itself more clearly.

What Happens Once a Slab Leak Is Suspected

Electronic leak detection, using acoustic tracing and sometimes thermal imaging, pinpoints the exact leak location to within a few inches before any concrete gets cut. That precision is what keeps the eventual repair small and targeted rather than an exploratory demolition of a larger area.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a slab leak go away on its own?
No, a slab leak does not self-repair and will continue eroding surrounding soil and potentially damaging flooring until it's professionally repaired.
Is a warm floor spot always a slab leak?
It's the most common cause, but radiant heating systems or nearby ductwork can occasionally produce a similar effect; a technician can confirm which applies.
How much does a slab leak typically cost to repair?
A single spot repair generally runs $500 to $1,500, while a full reroute avoiding the slab entirely runs $2,000 to $5,000, depending on the home's layout.
