What To Do When a Pipe Bursts: Kennesaw, GA Homeowners

By the Kennesaw Apex team

A burst pipe is one of the few plumbing situations where the first five minutes genuinely matter for how much damage the home takes. Knowing exactly what to do, and what not to do, before help arrives makes a real difference.

Step One: Shut Off The Water

The main shutoff valve is the single most important thing to locate before an emergency happens, not during one. In most Kennesaw homes on a slab foundation, it's near the water meter at the street or where the main line enters the house, often in a utility closet or garage. Turning this valve fully closed stops water flow to the entire house immediately, which matters more than trying to stop the leak at its exact source.

If the burst is isolated to one fixture, like a washing machine supply line, that fixture's individual shutoff valve is a faster option if it's easily accessible, but don't spend more than a few seconds looking for it before going straight to the main valve instead.

Step Two: Cut Power To The Affected Area

Water and electricity are a dangerous combination. If the burst pipe is anywhere near an outlet, appliance, or light fixture, and it's safe to reach the breaker panel without walking through standing water, shut off power to that specific circuit or the whole panel if there's any doubt about which circuit is affected.

If The Burst Happens While You're Away

A burst pipe discovered after returning from a trip presents a harder version of the same problem, since water has likely been running for hours or longer. The same first steps apply, shutting off the main valve immediately takes priority over assessing the damage.

A water leak detection sensor placed near a water heater or in a basement, inexpensive and simple to install, can catch this scenario early by sending a phone alert before a multi-day absence turns a burst pipe into a much larger loss.

Step Three: Start Removing Standing Water

Once the water is shut off and the area is electrically safe, removing standing water immediately limits how much gets absorbed into flooring, drywall, and framing. A wet vacuum, towels, or even buckets all help in the first several minutes while waiting for a plumber to arrive, and every gallon removed promptly is less water damage to deal with afterward.

What Not To Do

  • Do not use any electrical device or outlet in or near standing water.
  • Do not attempt to patch or tape the burst pipe under pressure. Wait until the water is shut off.
  • Do not delay calling a plumber while trying to fully dry the area yourself first.
  • Do not ignore a small trickle assuming it will stop. A stressed pipe section can fail further at any time.

The First 24 Hours Matter for More Than Cleanup

Beyond the immediate water damage, a burst pipe event is worth documenting even while dealing with the emergency itself. Photographing the damage, noting the approximate time the burst was discovered, and keeping any receipts for emergency mitigation supplies all matter if the damage ends up significant enough for an insurance claim. This documentation is easy to forget in the moment but far harder to reconstruct after the fact.

Common Causes Worth Knowing

A pipe rarely bursts for no reason. A hard freeze reaching an exposed or poorly insulated section is one common cause in Georgia specifically, since homes here aren't built with the same cold-weather protection as consistently colder climates.

Advanced corrosion in old galvanized pipe, where decades of internal rust have thinned the pipe wall to failure, is another. A sudden, sharp pressure spike, sometimes from a water hammer event when a valve closes too quickly, can also be enough to rupture an already-weakened section.

Knowing the likely cause doesn't change the immediate emergency response, but it does shape what a technician checks for nearby once the immediate repair is done, since a pipe that burst from age-related corrosion often has similarly aged, similarly stressed sections nearby worth a closer look.

Why Speed Matters More Than People Expect

A burst pipe running even briefly at full water pressure releases a significant volume of water very quickly, and the longer it runs, the more that water spreads into wall cavities, subfloor, and adjacent rooms rather than staying contained to the immediate area. This is also why mold risk starts within 24 to 48 hours in wet, warm conditions, making a fast response about more than just the immediate mess.

What Happens When The Plumber Arrives

A technician confirms the water is fully shut off, assesses the extent and cause of the break, and repairs or replaces the affected section. If the pipe burst from a known cause, like a frozen section during a cold snap or advanced corrosion in an old galvanized line, that gets explained, along with whether nearby sections of the same pipe run are at similar risk and worth addressing proactively.

If enough water escaped to affect flooring, drywall, or other materials, a separate water damage assessment covers the cleanup and drying process, which is a different scope of work than the plumbing repair itself.

Kennesaw Note: Find Your Whole-House Shutoff Before You Need It

Cobb Water's January 2024 freeze alert told customers to know how to shut off their water and to turn off the whole-house shutoff if a large quantity of water is running. The customer guide adds that the owner is responsible for piping on the home side of the meter.

That makes the shutoff inside your house the valve that matters in an emergency. Find it now, make sure it turns, and tell everyone in the household where it is.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where is the main water shutoff valve usually located?
In most Kennesaw homes, it's near the water meter at the street or where the main line enters the house, often in a garage, utility closet, or basement.
Is it safe to use a wet vacuum near a recent pipe burst?
Yes, once the water supply is fully shut off and any nearby electrical circuits are confirmed off, a wet vacuum is safe and helpful for removing standing water.
Can a burst pipe be a sign other pipes are about to fail too?
If the burst was caused by age-related corrosion in galvanized pipe, nearby sections of the same original plumbing run are worth having assessed, since they share the same age and material.
Should I shut off the water at the meter or inside the house?
Cobb Water's winter alert tells customers to use the whole-house shutoff when a large quantity of water is running. Find that valve ahead of time and check that it turns, since the home side of the meter is the owner's responsibility.
