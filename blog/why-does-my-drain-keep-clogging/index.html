Why Does My Drain Keep Clogging Even After Snaking?

By the Kennesaw Apex team

A drain that clears with snaking only to clog again a few weeks later is one of the more frustrating plumbing patterns, and it usually means the snake cleared a symptom without addressing what's actually coating the inside of the pipe.

What Snaking Actually Does

A mechanical drain snake works by physically breaking through or pulling back whatever is blocking the pipe at one specific point. It's fast and effective for a single, localized blockage, but it doesn't clean the rest of the pipe's interior surface. If the real problem is a buildup coating the pipe walls over a longer stretch, snaking restores flow immediately without removing what caused the clog in the first place.

Grease Buildup: The Most Common Repeat Offender

Kitchen drains that clog repeatedly almost always trace back to grease and cooking oil that solidifies as it cools inside the pipe. Even careful households that avoid pouring grease directly down the drain can see gradual buildup from smaller amounts washed off dishes and pans over months, and once enough has accumulated, the pipe's effective diameter narrows enough that even minor debris causes a new blockage.

A garbage disposal doesn't prevent this either, since it breaks food waste into smaller particles but doesn't change how grease itself behaves once it's in the pipe and cooling.

A Detail Worth Knowing About Tree Species

Not all trees pose the same root intrusion risk. Species with aggressive, water-seeking root systems, willows and certain fast-growing shade trees among them, are far more likely to find their way into a cracked sewer joint than slower-growing, deep-rooting species. A homeowner planning new landscaping near a known sewer line path can meaningfully reduce future risk simply by choosing tree species with less aggressive root behavior.

Root Intrusion In The Main Line

If it's not a kitchen line but the main sewer line that keeps backing up, especially if multiple fixtures are affected at once, tree roots finding their way into a cracked joint are a common cause, particularly in a home with mature trees near the sewer line's path. Roots grow aggressively toward the nutrient-rich environment inside a sewer line once they find an entry point, and they'll continue growing back again and again after simple removal unless the underlying structural crack is properly addressed.

A Cost Comparison Worth Understanding

Repeated snaking visits for the same recurring clog, each running $150 to $400, add up faster than most homeowners realize; three or four visits over a year easily exceeds the $400 to $800 cost of a single hydro jetting service that actually resolves the underlying buildup. Framed as a cumulative cost rather than a series of individual repair calls, jetting is often the more economical choice, not just the more thorough one.

Why Hydro Jetting Solves What Snaking Can't

Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water, typically 4,000 PSI or more, to scour the entire interior wall of the pipe rather than just punching through the blockage at one point. For a grease-coated kitchen line or a main line with root intrusion, this actually removes the buildup or root material along the full length of the pipe, rather than leaving a residue that quickly narrows the pipe again.

When a Camera Inspection Makes Sense First

Before jetting an older or unknown-condition line, a camera inspection confirms the pipe's actual structural condition and shows exactly where the buildup or root intrusion is concentrated. This also catches the difference between a line that just needs cleaning and one that has an actual structural crack requiring repair, which jetting alone wouldn't fix.

What About Chemical Drain Cleaners?

A chemical drain cleaner might clear a mild clog temporarily, but it doesn't remove the actual grease coating or root intrusion causing recurring problems, and repeated use can damage older pipe materials, particularly galvanized steel and aging PVC. For a drain that's already clogged multiple times, reaching for another bottle of chemical cleaner treats the same symptom again without addressing why it keeps happening, which is exactly the pattern that leads homeowners to call a plumber after months of temporary fixes.

Preventing The Next One

For kitchen lines, scraping plates into the trash before washing and avoiding pouring grease down the drain, even in small amounts, meaningfully slows future buildup. For a home with known root intrusion history, a maintenance jetting visit every year or two keeps the line clear proactively, which costs less over time and causes far less disruption than repeated emergency calls for the same recurring backup.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many times should I try snaking before considering jetting?
If the same drain clogs again within a few weeks of being professionally snaked, that's usually a strong enough signal that jetting is the better next step rather than snaking a third time.
Is hydro jetting safe for all pipe types?
It's safe for pipes in good structural condition; a camera inspection beforehand is recommended on older lines to confirm there's no existing damage the pressure could worsen.
Can I prevent grease buildup with hot water alone?
Running hot water after washing greasy dishes helps somewhat, but grease still resolidifies further down the pipe where it cools, so it's not a complete substitute for keeping grease out of the drain in the first place.
