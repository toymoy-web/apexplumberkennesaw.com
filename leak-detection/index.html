A water bill that has climbed with no change in household usage, the sound of running water when every fixture is off, or a damp patch with no obvious source are the classic signs of a hidden leak, and finding it accurately is the difference between a small repair and tearing open a wall unnecessarily.

If You Have An Active Leak Emergency Right Now

Water you can hear or see spreading deserves an immediate call, even when you cannot tell where it starts. These steps limit the damage while the 24/7 line gets a technician headed your way.

  1. Close the main shutoff, then open a faucet at the lowest point of the house to drain the pipes.
  2. Check the water meter with every fixture off. If the leak indicator or sweep hand still moves, the leak is on your side of the meter.
  3. Switch off power to any wet room and move rugs, boxes and electronics away from the damp area.
  4. Call (770) 214-4545 and tell the dispatcher what you hear, where it is damp, and whether the meter is moving.

When Leak Detection Is An Emergency, Not A Scheduled Visit

Some hidden leaks can wait for a morning appointment and some cannot. Water spreading across a floor, a ceiling that sags or drips, a meter that spins with every fixture off, or a wet patch in the yard with no rain all belong in the emergency category.

A leak that has already forced a shutoff is the clearest case. With the water off the household is stuck, and every hour the line stays open the subfloor, drywall and insulation soak up more. Calling at night is reasonable here because tracing can begin the moment a technician arrives.

Guessing at a leak's location and opening a wall on a hunch is exactly what proper detection equipment is meant to prevent, and it's the single biggest difference between a targeted repair and unnecessary, costly demolition work.

How A Hidden Leak Gets Found

A technician starts by isolating sections of the plumbing system to narrow down whether the leak is on the supply side or a fixture, then uses acoustic listening equipment to trace the sound of escaping water through walls, floors, or slab. Thermal imaging helps on hot water line leaks specifically, since the temperature difference shows up on camera before any visible sign appears.

Ruling Out False Positives First

A running toilet, an irrigation system left partially open, or an ice maker line with a small drip can all cause the same rising water bill a real hidden leak would, and checking these first saves an unnecessary detection visit. A technician typically confirms the meter is still moving with every fixture and the irrigation system off before assuming the leak is inside the home's structure.

Why Slab Homes Make This Trickier

A leak under a slab foundation, common across Kennesaw, gives no visual clue the way a leak in an accessible crawl space might, which is exactly why electronic tracing matters more here than in areas with more basement or crawl-space construction. The detection step isn't optional groundwork, it's the only reliable way to avoid cutting into the wrong section of a slab.

Most detection visits are completed within two to three hours, and if the leak turns out to be repairable in the same visit, the technician will present that option before packing up rather than requiring a separate appointment.

Common Places Leaks Hide

Behind a water heater, inside a wall shared with a bathroom, under a slab foundation, or at an underground segment of the water service line between the meter and the house are the most common hiding spots. Each location points toward a different confirmation method, which is why the tracing step happens before anyone opens a wall.

Tracing Hidden Leaks Near Kennesaw Mountain

A water bill that climbs with nothing visibly wet is a common reason people call from Stilesboro Ridge, Legacy Park, and the streets that run toward Kennesaw Mountain National Battlefield Park. Irrigation lines and slab supply lines both hide well in this clay, so the technician checks the meter, the irrigation zones, and the slab before opening a wall.

A Quick Check Before You Call

  • Turn off every fixture and appliance that uses water, then check the water meter for movement.
  • If the meter is still moving, the leak is on your side of the line.
  • Note any warm spots on the floor or unexplained damp patches.

A leak confirmed to be under a slab foundation specifically gets full treatment on the slab leak detection and repair page. A leak traced to old galvanized or polybutylene supply lines throughout the home points toward repiping instead of a one-off repair, since the underlying material is the real issue. Acworth's historic downtown core shows this same aging-pipe leak pattern.

When The Water Meter Keeps Moving With Everything Off

A meter that creeps forward after every tap, appliance and irrigation zone is shut off is the most reliable sign of a leak past the meter. Kennesaw homes get water through Cobb County Water System, and its customer guide says the property owner is responsible for the piping and fixtures on the home side of the meter.

A bill that jumps with no change in habits tells the same story from another angle. The technician starts at the meter, separates the house from the irrigation and the water heater, and works out whether the loss is inside the structure, under the slab or in the yard.

Leaks Between The Meter And The House

The service line that runs from the meter to the house is buried, so a leak there rarely shows up as a puddle. Warning signs include a patch of lawn that stays soft or greener than the rest, a sudden drop in pressure, a meter that runs while the house is dry, and water surfacing near the meter box.

Ground-listening and line-tracing equipment marks the leak within a small area. That means a repair can open one section of yard instead of trenching the whole run. When the whole line is old, replacing it may make more sense than patching, which the repiping page explains.

Ceiling Stains And Leaks Inside Walls

A brown stain on a ceiling, a bubbling patch of paint, or a damp wall below a bathroom usually means a pipe is leaking above or inside the surface. The source is often not where the stain appears, since water travels along joists and pipes before it shows.

Thermal imaging, moisture readings and acoustic tracing narrow the source before any drywall comes down. If the area is already wet, the water damage restoration team can dry it out once the leak is found and fixed.

Irrigation Lines, Pools And Spas

Irrigation systems hide leaks well. A zone that runs with unusually fast flow, a soggy spot that never dries, or a bill that rises in the watering season often points to a broken head, a cracked lateral or a failing valve.

Pools, spas and hot tubs add supply and return lines of their own, and a technician can isolate them from the house and test them separately. Irrigation and pool supply lines often carry backflow devices too, and the backflow testing team handles the annual test that goes with them.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can you find a leak without seeing any water?
Acoustic tracing picks up the sound of water escaping under pressure, and thermal imaging catches temperature differences on hot water lines, both well before a leak becomes visible.
How much water loss actually shows up on a bill?
Even a small pinhole leak can waste hundreds of gallons a month, which is often enough to cause a noticeable jump in a water bill before any visible sign appears.
Is the meter test reliable?
Yes, if the meter continues moving with every fixture off, there is an active leak somewhere on the home's side of the water line.
How accurate is acoustic leak detection?
In experienced hands, acoustic and thermal tracing typically pinpoint a leak within a few inches, which is what keeps the eventual repair small and targeted rather than exploratory.
Can a leak be intermittent and still be detected?
Yes, isolating sections of the plumbing system and monitoring the meter over time can catch a leak that only shows up under certain pressure or temperature conditions.
Can you trace a leak at night or on a weekend in Kennesaw?
Yes. The line is open 24/7 including weekends and holidays, and electronic tracing equipment goes out on emergency calls the same as scheduled ones. The dispatcher confirms timing based on current call volume.
Can you find a leak in an irrigation line or a pool supply line?
Yes. The technician isolates the irrigation or pool line from the house, then tests and traces that section on its own. Mention the zone or equipment involved when you call.
Do you detect leaks in rentals and small multi-unit buildings?
Yes. Leaks in duplexes, triplexes and small apartment buildings are traced the same way, starting at the meter and working through the units. Tell the dispatcher how many units share the supply.
What should I tell the dispatcher about a high water bill?
Share when the bill started climbing, whether the meter moves with everything off, and where you have noticed damp spots or sounds. Those details help the technician choose where to start.
