Grease Trap Overdue Or Backing Up?

☎ Call Now

A commercial kitchen's grease trap that's overdue for cleaning shows up as slow-draining sinks, a foul odor near the trap, or in the worst case, a backup during service hours. Scheduled cleaning on a set interval avoids all of these and keeps a restaurant compliant with local health code.

A backup during dinner service is the scenario every kitchen manager wants to avoid, and it's almost always preventable with a cleaning schedule matched to the kitchen's real volume rather than a generic calendar default that ignores how busy a given kitchen actually runs.

A first visit typically includes assessing current usage patterns to recommend a realistic interval, rather than defaulting to a generic quarterly schedule that may not fit the kitchen's actual daily output.

Why Grease Traps Need Regular Service

A grease trap works by slowing wastewater flow long enough for fats, oils, and grease to separate and float to the top, where they're trapped rather than flowing into the municipal sewer line. Once the trap fills to about 25% capacity with trapped grease, it stops functioning effectively and grease starts passing through into the line, risking a code violation and a much larger backup down the line.

Setting The Right Interval

A high-volume kitchen, like a fryer-heavy restaurant, typically needs service every 4 to 6 weeks, while a lower-volume operation might stretch to every 2 to 3 months. The right interval is set based on actual kitchen volume and trap size, not a generic default.

Kitchen Practices That Extend The Interval

Scraping plates and pans into the trash before washing, rather than letting food scraps and grease go down the drain, meaningfully slows how fast a trap fills. A dry-wipe step before washing heavily greased pans catches a surprising amount of grease that would otherwise end up in the trap, stretching the time between required cleanings.

Serving Kennesaw's Restaurant Corridor

Restaurants along Kennesaw's main commercial corridors range from quick-service fryer-heavy operations to full-service kitchens with lower grease volume, and the right cleaning interval reflects that real difference in usage rather than a one-size-fits-all schedule applied across every account.

A standard cleaning visit, including pumping out the trap and documenting the service for compliance records, typically takes 30 to 45 minutes and can be scheduled on a recurring basis to match the kitchen's actual grease volume.

Compliance Basics

  • Most local health codes require documented grease trap maintenance records kept on file and available for inspection.
  • A trap at roughly 25% capacity or more needs cleaning regardless of what the calendar schedule says.
  • Repeated code violations for grease discharge into the municipal line can result in fines or a mandated more-frequent inspection cycle.

A broader commercial plumbing issue beyond the grease trap itself is covered on the commercial plumbing page for restaurants and other kitchen-equipped properties. Drain clogs in the kitchen that persist even after grease trap service point toward hydro jetting for the line itself, since buildup inside the pipe walls needs a different fix than the trap. Frequently Asked Questions

How often does a restaurant grease trap need cleaning in Georgia?
It depends on kitchen volume, but most commercial kitchens need service every 4 to 12 weeks to stay ahead of code violations and backups.
What happens if a grease trap isn't cleaned on schedule?
Grease starts passing into the municipal sewer line, which risks a code violation, fines, and eventually a backup that can shut down kitchen operations.
Do you provide documentation for health inspections?
Yes, service records are provided that document each cleaning date, useful for health department compliance checks.
Can grease trap cleaning be scheduled outside business hours?
Yes, early morning or after-close scheduling is available specifically so cleaning doesn't disrupt kitchen operations during service hours.
What size grease trap does a typical restaurant need?
Sizing depends on kitchen output and local code requirements; a technician can assess whether an existing trap is undersized for current kitchen volume.
Do grease trap additives replace the need for pumping?
No, enzyme or bacterial additives can help break down some grease between services, but they don't eliminate the need for scheduled physical cleaning and disposal.
