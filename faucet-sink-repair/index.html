A dripping faucet wastes more water over a year than most people expect, and a leak under the sink, even a slow one, can damage the cabinet floor before it's noticed. Both are usually quick, inexpensive fixes.

If A Faucet Or Supply Line Has Burst

A failed supply line can empty a lot of water into a cabinet in minutes. Stopping the flow comes first and the repair second.

  1. Turn the angle stop under the sink clockwise to close it. If it will not turn, close the main shutoff instead.
  2. Set towels and a bucket under the leak and empty the cabinet so nothing soaks up water.
  3. Do not run the dishwasher or disposal until the leak is fixed.
  4. Call (770) 214-4545 and describe where the water is coming from, whether the faucet, the stop valve or the wall.

When A Faucet Problem Turns Into An Emergency

A drip can wait. A cracked faucet body spraying water, a supply line that has split, a handle that comes off in your hand, or water running from the wall behind a fixture cannot.

The most common reason a small repair becomes urgent is a shutoff valve that will not close. Old stop valves seize over time, and trying to force them can break the stem and open the line fully.

Most calls fall into one of these two categories, and knowing which one applies before the visit helps a technician bring the right replacement parts and tools on the first trip rather than needing a separate follow-up visit later on.

What Causes A Dripping Faucet

Modern faucets use a ceramic disc or cartridge to control flow, and that part wears out over years of use, especially in homes with harder municipal water where mineral buildup accelerates the wear. Older faucet designs with rubber washers fail the same way, just from simple wear rather than mineral buildup.

What Causes A Leak Under The Sink

A supply line connection that has loosened, a corroded compression fitting, or a P-trap that has shifted out of alignment are the most common sources. Because these leaks often happen slowly, they're frequently found because of water-stained cabinet flooring rather than visible dripping.

When Replacement Beats Repair

A faucet more than 15 years old with a discontinued cartridge, or one that's already had two or more repairs, usually costs about the same to replace as to keep repairing, and a new installation comes with a fresh warranty. A faucet under 10 years old with a single worn part is almost always the better repair candidate.

Mineral Buildup And Faucet Life

Mineral buildup inside a faucet cartridge shortens its life, and how fast that happens depends on the hardness of the water at your tap. A hardness test strip gives a quick read, and a whole-home filtration system slows this wear across every faucet in the house at once, rather than just the one currently dripping.

Most faucet and sink repairs are completed within an hour, and a technician carries dozens of common cartridges and supply line fittings on the truck to avoid a parts-sourcing delay.

What To Check Before You Call

  • Look for water staining or warping on the cabinet floor even if you haven't noticed active dripping recently.
  • Check whether the leak is coming from the faucet body itself or from a supply line connection underneath the sink.
  • Note the faucet brand and approximate age if visible, since that speeds up parts sourcing and helps decide between repair and replacement.

A leak under the sink that's actually part of a broader low-pressure or discoloration pattern throughout the home points toward repiping rather than a single fixture repair. A garbage disposal leaking at the same sink is covered separately on the garbage disposal repair and installation page, since the two often get confused during an initial phone description. Acworth's older subdivisions see this same fixture-wear pattern.

Showers, Tubs And Valve Cartridges

Dripping tub spouts, showerheads that leak behind the handle, and valves that no longer shut off all share a cause, a worn cartridge or seat. Replacing the cartridge restores the seal without replacing the whole valve in many cases.

If the valve sits behind a tiled wall, access matters. The technician tells you whether the fix can be done through the trim opening or whether a short access cut in the wall is needed.

Outdoor Spigots And Hose Bibs

An outdoor spigot that drips, or that leaks inside the wall when the hose is on, usually needs a new washer or a replacement bib. Frost-proof designs hide the shutoff inside the wall, so a split in the stem may not show until water appears indoors.

Georgia's state amendments to the plumbing code, as revised in January 2024, call for at least two exterior hose bibs on one- and two-family homes of three stories or fewer. Most Kennesaw houses therefore have more than one to check before winter.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my faucet still drip after I tighten the handle?
Tightening the handle doesn't fix a worn internal cartridge or washer, which is almost always the actual cause of a persistent drip.
Is a slow drip really worth fixing right away?
A faucet dripping once per second wastes over 3,000 gallons a year, so yes, it's worth fixing even though it seems minor.
Can you match my existing faucet finish if only part needs replacing?
For a cartridge or internal part replacement, yes. For a full faucet swap, matching the exact finish depends on whether the original model is still in production.
Do you install customer-supplied faucets?
Yes, a customer-supplied faucet can be installed for a labor-only rate, provided it's a standard configuration compatible with the existing supply line spacing.
Can a single-handle faucet be converted to two-handle, or vice versa?
It depends on the existing sink's hole configuration. A technician confirms compatibility before recommending a specific replacement model.
Does a dripping faucet always mean the whole faucet needs replacing?
No, most drips trace to a single worn cartridge or washer, both inexpensive parts that fix the problem without replacing the entire fixture.
What if the shutoff under my sink will not close?
Close the main shutoff and call. A seized stop valve is a common reason a small fix becomes an emergency, and a technician can replace the valve once the water is off.
Do you repair frost-proof hose bibs?
Yes. A technician can replace the washer, the stem or the whole bib. If water appears inside the wall when the hose is on, close the shutoff for that line and call.
Can you replace a leaking shower valve or tub spout?
Yes. Cartridge and valve replacements are routine, and the technician explains whether the repair can be done from the trim side or needs access behind the wall.
