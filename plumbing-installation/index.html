New plumbing installation covers everything from full rough-in work on new construction to adding or relocating fixtures during a kitchen or bathroom remodel, all done to the International Plumbing Code as adopted with Georgia's state amendments.

Whether the project is a single addition or a full new build, the same code-compliance standard applies, and getting an inspection-ready result the first time avoids costly delays later in the project.

A failed inspection due to a rough-in mistake is far more expensive to fix once walls are closed than catching it before that stage.

New Construction Rough-In

Rough-in plumbing happens before walls are closed up, setting the supply and drain lines for every fixture in the home. Getting this stage right the first time matters more than almost any other part of a build, since correcting a rough-in mistake after drywall goes up is far more expensive than doing it correctly during the initial install.

Remodel Plumbing

Moving a sink, adding a second bathroom, or relocating a laundry hookup all require extending or rerouting existing supply and drain lines, which is a different scope than rough-in on an empty structure. Working around existing walls, framing, and fixtures takes more planning but follows the same code requirements.

Coordinating With Your General Contractor

Rough-in plumbing has to happen at a specific point in a build's timeline, after framing but before insulation and drywall, and it needs to pass inspection before the walls close up. Coordinating that timing with a general contractor's schedule avoids the costly scenario of drywall going up before an inspector has signed off on the plumbing behind it.

Building On Kennesaw's Slab Foundations

New construction on a slab foundation, the norm across most of Kennesaw, requires under-slab plumbing to be roughed in and pressure-tested before the concrete pour, since any mistake at that stage means breaking concrete to fix it later. Getting that under-slab layout right the first time is worth the extra planning time compared to a crawl-space or basement foundation where later access is at least possible.

An initial consultation reviews the project plans and fixture count to provide a scoped estimate, and larger projects are scheduled in coordination with the broader construction timeline rather than as a single drop-in visit.

New Plumbing For Kennesaw Remodels And Infill

Kennesaw keeps seeing remodels and infill work around downtown Big Shanty and near Town Center, from kitchen and bath renovations in older houses to new fixtures in townhomes. New plumbing in an older house means tying into existing galvanized or cast iron lines carefully, and permits are pulled where Cobb County or the city requires them.

What To Have Ready Before The Estimate

  • Floor plans or drawings showing exactly where each planned fixture will sit in the finished space.
  • Whether the project is new construction, an addition, or a remodel of already-existing space, since each has different access considerations.
  • Any specific fixture models already chosen, since rough-in dimensions and connection requirements vary meaningfully between fixture models.

A single fixture repair or replacement, rather than new installation as part of a larger project, is better covered on the fixture's own page, like toilet repair and installation or faucet and sink repair. Mableton's rapid post-2000 growth generates the most new-construction plumbing calls in this network.

Showers, Tubs And Kitchen Sinks

A new shower, a replaced tub or a relocated kitchen sink all need supply and drain lines that fit the new layout. The plan covers valve placement, drain size and venting before any tile goes up.

Doing the plumbing before the surface work avoids tearing out finished walls. Share the fixtures and layout when you call so the technician can bring the right valves and fittings.

Washer Boxes And Appliance Connections

Washer boxes, ice maker lines and similar appliance connections need correct supply and drain details. A standpipe that is too short or a missing drain can lead to an overflow the first time the machine runs.

The technician sets the valves and the drain, then tests under load before leaving.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you handle full new construction plumbing?
Yes, including full rough-in for new homes or additions, done to Georgia's adopted plumbing and fuel gas codes.
Can plumbing be added to a room that didn't have it before?
In most cases yes, though the cost and complexity depend on how far the new fixture location is from existing supply and drain lines.
Do I need a permit for remodel plumbing work?
Most plumbing work beyond simple fixture swaps requires a permit under Georgia's adopted plumbing code. A licensed plumber typically handles the permit filing as part of the job.
Can you work alongside my contractor or designer on a remodel?
Yes, coordinating directly with a general contractor or designer on timing and fixture specifications is standard practice for remodel and new construction plumbing.
How far in advance should plumbing be scheduled for a new build?
Rough-in plumbing should be scheduled to align with the framing completion date, typically coordinated a few weeks ahead as part of the overall construction timeline.
Can plumbing fixtures be upgraded mid-project if plans change?
Yes, though changes are easiest and cheapest before rough-in is finalized. A fixture swap after rough-in may require adjusting the supply and drain locations already set.
Can you reconnect a sink after countertop work?
Yes. Disconnecting and reconnecting a sink, faucet and disposal is routine. The technician tests the supply lines and the drain for leaks before leaving.
Can you install a tub or shower during a bathroom remodel?
Yes. Tub and shower installations are part of remodel plumbing, and they work best when scheduled with the framing and tile stages.
