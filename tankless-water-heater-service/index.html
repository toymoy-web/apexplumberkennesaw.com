A tankless water heater that is throwing an error code, delivering lukewarm water, or making a rattling sound during operation almost always needs a descale service rather than a full replacement, and mineral scale on the heat exchanger is the most common cause.

Why Tankless Units Need Regular Service

A tankless unit heats water on demand by running it across a heat exchanger, and mineral deposits from hard water build up on that exchanger over time. Once enough scale accumulates, the unit's flow sensor reads incorrectly, triggering an error code even though nothing has actually broken.

Manufacturers generally call for a periodic descale flush, and the right interval depends on the water hardness at your tap and how much hot water the home uses. Skipping that service is a common reason a tankless unit fails years earlier than its rated lifespan.

What The Descale Service Involves

A technician isolates the unit with service valves, circulates a descaling solution through the heat exchanger for roughly 45 minutes, then flushes it clean. The whole visit typically takes under two hours and restores full flow rate and heating performance.

If the unit is still under-performing after a descale, the next most likely cause is a failing flow sensor or igniter, both of which are repairable without replacing the whole unit.

Gas vs. Electric Tankless Units

A gas tankless unit produces higher flow rates and handles whole-home demand better, but needs proper venting and, in some cases, an upgraded gas line, both governed by the International Fuel Gas Code as adopted with Georgia's amendments. An electric tankless unit skips the venting requirement entirely but often needs a dedicated high-amp electrical circuit instead.

Either type benefits equally from regular descaling, since the mineral scale buildup that causes most service calls forms inside the heat exchanger regardless of the power source.

Water Hardness And Service Intervals

Cobb Water's 2023 water quality report names the Chattahoochee River and Lake Allatoona as its sources but does not list a hardness figure, so the amount of scale a unit collects depends on the home and its usage.

A hardness test strip or a lab test tells you where your water falls, and the technician can set a descale interval from that result. A home with a whole-home softener can often stretch the interval, since the softener removes most of the mineral content before it reaches the unit.

Most service calls, including diagnosis and the descale flush itself, are completed within a single two-hour visit, and a technician confirms the fix by testing hot water temperature and flow rate before leaving.

Tankless Units In Newer Kennesaw Communities

Tankless heaters tend to appear in newer builds and remodels in places like Legacy Park, Barrett Lakes, and the newer sections near Town Center, where the gas line and vent were planned around them. Scale still builds on the heat exchanger over time, so periodic descaling matters in these communities just as it does in the older homes downtown.

  • Note the exact error code displayed on the unit's screen or remote.
  • Check that the gas supply valve to the unit is fully open.
  • Confirm the outdoor vent termination isn't blocked by debris.

Error Codes, Burnt Wiring And Failed Igniters

Tankless units show a numbered code when a sensor, igniter or flame problem stops the burner. A code that clears after a reset and returns a few days later is a sign of scale, a weak sensor or a part nearing failure.

The technician reads the code, inspects the burner area and tests the sensors. Burned wiring or a failed thermocouple is repaired by replacing the part and then testing a full heating cycle.

Flush Only, Repair Or Replace

Plenty of owners just want the unit flushed and do not want a replacement pitch. A flush and descale restores flow and heat transfer on a unit that is otherwise working, and it is the right first step when the only symptom is lukewarm water.

If the heat exchanger is cracked or the unit is past repair, the technician says so and explains the alternatives. The water heater installation and replacement page covers moving to a new tankless unit or back to a tank.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should a tankless water heater be descaled?
The right interval depends on your water hardness and usage, and many owners descale about once a year. A hardness test shows where your home falls, and homes with a whole-home water softener can often stretch the interval.
Can I descale a tankless unit myself?
A basic vinegar flush kit exists for DIY use, but a professional service isolates the unit correctly and checks the flow sensor and venting at the same time, catching issues a DIY flush would miss.
What does it mean if my tankless unit shows an error code after a power outage?
Most units need a manual reset after a power interruption. If the code persists after resetting, the control board or ignition system likely needs inspection.
Does a tankless unit need annual service even without an error code?
A yearly or every-18-month descale is recommended as preventive maintenance regardless of whether an error code has appeared, since scale builds up before it triggers a fault.
Which tankless brands do you service?
Rinnai, Navien, Rheem, and Noritz make up the large majority of units serviced in the Kennesaw area.
Does a tankless unit really provide truly unlimited hot water?
It provides continuous hot water within the unit's flow rate limit, meaning multiple fixtures running at once can still exceed capacity. Sizing correctly for the home's peak simultaneous demand avoids this.
Can you service a tankless unit that someone else installed?
In most cases yes. Bring the model number, and the technician checks that the unit can be serviced and that parts are available. Brand-specific work depends on the model.
