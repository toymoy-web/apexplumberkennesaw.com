A toilet that runs constantly, flushes weakly, or rocks slightly at the base each has a specific, usually inexpensive cause, and most repairs are completed in a single visit without replacing the whole toilet.

If A Toilet Is Overflowing Or Leaking

An overflowing toilet can soak a bathroom floor in minutes. Stop the flow first and decide on the repair after.

  1. Lift the tank lid and press the flapper down or lift the float to stop the refill.
  2. Turn the supply valve behind the toilet clockwise. If it will not close, shut the main.
  3. Do not flush again, and mop standing water before it reaches the hallway or a ceiling below.
  4. Call (770) 214-4545 and say whether it is overflowing, leaking at the base or simply not flushing.

When A Toilet Problem Is An Emergency

An overflow that will not stop, a leak at the base that is soaking the subfloor, and a toilet that backs up when other fixtures drain all need quick attention. A home with a single bathroom also treats a dead toilet as an emergency.

Gurgling in the toilet after the shower drains usually points past the toilet to the drain line. The technician checks both so the problem does not return.

Each symptom points toward a different worn part, so an accurate diagnosis up front avoids paying for a repair that doesn't actually address the real underlying problem.

What's Usually Wrong

A running toilet is almost always a worn flapper or a fill valve that isn't shutting off completely, both simple parts to replace. A weak flush usually traces to a partially clogged jet ring or, on older low-flow models from the early 2000s, an undersized flush design that was never very effective to begin with.

A toilet that rocks at the base signals a failing wax ring seal underneath, which needs to be replaced before it leaks onto the subfloor and causes real water damage.

High-Efficiency Toilet Options

Georgia's plumbing code amendments define specific high-efficiency standards, including dual-flush models rated under the WaterSense Tank-Type High Efficiency Toilet Specification, which use as little as 1.28 gallons per flush on average across both flush settings. Upgrading a pre-2000 low-flow toilet to a modern WaterSense-rated model often improves flush performance while cutting water use, addressing two problems with one installation.

Older Kennesaw Homes And Original Toilets

A number of Kennesaw homes built before the 1994 federal low-flow mandate still have their original toilets, which use significantly more water per flush than anything sold today and often show their age through cracked porcelain or a tank that no longer holds a consistent water level. Replacing one of these older units is usually a bigger practical upgrade than replacing an already-compliant toilet from the 2000s.

Most repairs and installations are completed within one to two hours, and a technician carries common replacement parts and standard toilet models on the truck so a same-day fix rarely depends on a return trip.

When Replacement Makes More Sense

A toilet older than 20 years, one that has needed several repairs already, or one of the original low-flow models with a genuinely weak flush design is usually worth replacing rather than continuing to repair. A new installation also gets a fresh wax ring and properly torqued bolts, eliminating the base leak risk in the same visit.

Toilets In Student Rentals And Family Homes Alike

Around Kennesaw State University and the apartments and condos along Chastain Road, the repeat calls are running toilets and worn flappers from heavy use. Family homes in Ellison Lakes and Heritage Park lean more toward replacements for water savings or comfort height, and both kinds of call usually end the same visit.

What To Check Before You Call

  • Jiggle the handle first. Sometimes the chain has just come loose.
  • Check if the toilet rocks slightly when you sit on it, which points to a wax ring issue.
  • Note whether the water level in the tank looks unusually high or low.

A toilet leak that has gone unnoticed long enough to cause floor damage should also see the water damage restoration page. A whole-house low-pressure issue affecting the toilet along with everything else points toward repiping rather than a toilet-specific fix. Powder Springs' original 1960s ranch-home toilets show this same age-related wear.

Leaks At The Base, Wax Rings And Loose Toilets

Water around the base of a toilet usually means the wax ring has failed or the toilet has loosened on the flange. The leak is small at first but soaks the subfloor over time, and it is easy to miss under a rug.

Replacing the wax ring and resetting the toilet fixes most of these. If the flange is cracked or the subfloor is soft, the technician tells you before reinstalling the toilet.

Running Toilets, Fill Valves And Supply Valves

A toilet that runs on its own, refills every few minutes, or hisses all night is wasting water. A worn flapper, a failed fill valve or a leaking supply valve are the usual causes, and most can be replaced in one visit.

A supply valve that drips behind the toilet is worth repairing early, because a stiff valve may fail when you finally need to turn it. If two bathrooms are running, check both, since a small leak in each adds up.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my toilet keep running after I jiggle the handle?
A worn flapper that isn't sealing correctly is the most common cause, and it's a low-cost part to replace.
How long does a new toilet installation take?
Most installations, including removal of the old unit, take 1 to 2 hours.
Is a rocking toilet dangerous to keep using?
It won't fail suddenly, but the wax ring seal underneath is likely compromised, and continuing to use it risks a slow leak onto the subfloor that causes real damage over time.
Can a toilet be reset without replacing it entirely?
Yes, resetting the existing toilet with a new wax ring is standard practice when the toilet itself is in good condition and only the seal has failed.
Do all toilets use the same size wax ring?
No, there are standard and extra-thick options depending on the flange height and floor material, which a technician confirms during the visit.
Is it normal for a toilet to need a repair every year or two?
No. A toilet needing frequent repairs, especially the same issue recurring, usually signals it's time to replace rather than keep repairing an aging unit.
What if my only toilet stops working at night?
Call the 24/7 line. A technician can come out for a stuck flush valve, a clog, a failed fill valve or a leak at the base, and will explain whether a repair or a replacement makes sense.
Can you replace a broken flush lever or handle?
Yes. Flush levers and handles are quick repairs on most tanks. Some one-piece models need a specific part, so the make and model help.
Can you swap round-bowl toilets for elongated ones?
Often yes. The technician measures the rough-in distance and the space in front of the bowl first, because elongated bowls need more clearance.
