If Your Water Heater Is Leaking Or Flooding

A leaking tank can put a lot of water on the floor quickly. These steps contain it while you wait for a technician.

  1. Turn off the power at the breaker or set the gas control to off or pilot.
  2. Close the cold water inlet valve on top of the heater to stop new water from entering the tank.
  3. Clear stored items away from the heater and mop standing water before it spreads to carpet or drywall.
  4. Call (770) 214-4545 and tell the dispatcher the tank size, the fuel type and where the heater sits.

When No Hot Water Is An Emergency

A heater that is cold in the middle of the night is not always an emergency, but a leaking tank, a flooded closet, rusty water coming out of the hot tap, or a family without hot water in winter usually is. Water heaters in garages and closets can leak for hours before anyone notices.

If you smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call 911 or the gas utility before you call us. Gas safety comes before any repair.

Signs Your Water Heater Needs Repair, Not Replacement

Cold or lukewarm water from every tap usually points to a failed heating element or a bad thermostat rather than a dying tank, and both are repairable in most cases. A puddle at the base of the tank is a different signal and often means the tank itself has started to corrode from the inside.

A popping or rumbling sound during heating cycles is sediment buildup, common in Cobb County's harder municipal water, and it is fixable with a flush before it turns into a cracked tank. Rusty or metallic-tasting hot water, on the other hand, usually means the anode rod or the tank lining has already failed.

What Causes These Failures in Kennesaw Homes

Homes on Kennesaw's slab foundations often keep the water heater in a garage or a small interior closet with limited ventilation, which shortens the working life of both the heating element and the tank itself. Older units installed before 2010 are also past the point where most manufacturers still stock exact replacement parts, which pushes some repairs toward replacement instead.

How We Diagnose the Problem

A technician checks the thermostat and heating element with a multimeter first, since that combination explains most no-hot-water calls. If the tank itself shows visible corrosion or an active leak at a weld seam, that is a replacement conversation rather than a repair, and the plumber will explain the real cost difference on the spot before doing any work.

Repair vs. Replace

A unit under six years old with a single failed part is almost always worth repairing. A unit past ten years, or one that has already needed two repairs in the past year, usually costs less to replace than to keep patching. See the full breakdown on the water heater installation and replacement page if a new unit looks like the better math.

What To Check Before You Call

  • Confirm the breaker or gas shutoff valve for the unit has not been tripped or closed.
  • Check whether the pilot light is lit on a gas unit before assuming the control valve has failed.
  • Look under the tank for standing water, which changes this from a repair call to an active leak.

If the diagnosis points to a full failure rather than a fixable part, the same visit can cover installation of a new unit, and a technician carrying signs of a slab leak nearby will also flag it, since slab leak detection and water heater failures share some of the same red-clay-driven causes in Kennesaw's older homes. Homeowners near Acworth and Powder Springs see the same brand mix and repair patterns, since both areas share Kennesaw's housing-era profile.

Gas vs. Electric Water Heater Repair

Gas units fail more often at the thermocouple, pilot assembly, or gas control valve, and a plumber checks for a clogged burner orifice before assuming the valve itself is bad. Electric units fail more often at one of the two heating elements or at the upper thermostat, and testing both with a multimeter takes only a few minutes.

Either type can also fail from sediment buildup in Cobb County's harder municipal water, which insulates the heating element or burner from the water it is supposed to heat and forces the unit to run longer than it should. A yearly flush is the most effective way to prevent this specific failure.

When a Repair Call Turns Into a Replacement Conversation

A tank that has already been repaired twice in the same year is telling you something a third repair will not fix. Once a plumber sees corrosion at a weld seam, rust-colored water from the hot tap in every fixture, or a tank that is more than ten years old, the honest recommendation shifts from repair to replacement, and the cost difference gets explained on the spot rather than after the work starts.

Leaks From The Tank, The Fittings Or The Relief Valve

Not every puddle near a water heater means a failed tank. A dripping fitting, a weeping relief valve or a loose connection on the cold inlet can all leave water on the floor while the tank itself is still sound.

A leak from the body of the tank is different. Corrosion has opened the steel, and no repair will close it, so the conversation moves to replacement. The technician checks each possible source before saying which one it is.

No Hot Water Or Not Enough Of It

A heater that has gone cold usually has a failed heating element, a bad thermostat, a gas valve, a pilot or igniter, or a tripped safety control. Running out of hot water too fast often points to sediment or a failed dip tube.

On gas units the technician checks the pilot assembly and venting, and on electric units the elements and thermostats. Many of these repairs are finished in one visit when the parts are on hand.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is water heater repair covered by homeowners insurance?
Insurance typically covers sudden tank rupture damage to the surrounding area but not the repair or replacement cost of the unit itself.
How long does a water heater repair take?
Most repairs, including a heating element, thermostat, or anode rod replacement, are completed in a single same-day visit.
Can a water heater be repaired instead of replaced?
A unit under six years old with one failed part is usually a good repair candidate. A unit over ten years old, or one that has failed twice in a year, often costs less to replace.
Do you repair water heaters after hours in Kennesaw?
Yes. Leaks, no-heat failures and tripped safety controls are handled on the 24/7 line. A technician diagnoses the unit first and explains whether a repair makes sense or whether replacement is the better call.
How do I tell whether the water is coming from the heater or a pipe nearby?
Dry the area, then check the tank, the pipes above it and the cold inlet for moisture over the next hour. Water on the tank body points to the heater, while drips from a pipe above point to a fitting or supply line. Tell the technician what you found.
Do you relight a gas water heater that went out?
A technician can relight the pilot and test the unit. If the pilot will not stay lit, that points to a failed thermocouple, a gas valve or a venting problem, and the repair follows from that.
