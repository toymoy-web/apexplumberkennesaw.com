A backflow prevention device stops contaminated water from flowing backward into the clean municipal supply, and many Kennesaw properties, particularly those with irrigation systems or certain commercial configurations, are required to have that device tested annually.

Missing a scheduled annual test isn't just a compliance issue, it also means a genuine safety mechanism may be sitting unverified for another full year without anyone noticing it has quietly failed.

Property owners are typically notified ahead of their test due date, and scheduling early avoids the risk of a lapsed compliance window that some water authorities flag automatically, sometimes restricting service until it's resolved and documentation is filed correctly.

Why Backflow Prevention Matters

Under normal conditions, water flows one direction: from the municipal supply into the home or property. A sudden drop in municipal pressure, like during a water main break or heavy firefighting demand nearby, can reverse that flow, potentially pulling contaminated water from an irrigation system or industrial connection back into the clean water supply. A backflow preventer is a mechanical safeguard against exactly that scenario.

What Annual Testing Involves

A certified test checks that the device's internal check valves are sealing properly and that the relief valve activates correctly under a pressure differential. The results are documented and typically need to be filed with the local water authority, which is why the paperwork matters as much as the mechanical test itself.

Types Of Backflow Preventers

A reduced pressure zone (RPZ) assembly offers the highest level of protection and is typically required for higher-hazard connections, like commercial irrigation with chemical fertilizer injection. A double check valve assembly offers a lower but often sufficient level of protection for standard residential irrigation systems. Which type a property needs depends on the specific hazard level of what's connected to the water line.

Backflow Requirements Around Kennesaw

Cobb County Water System requires backflow prevention on most irrigation systems and many commercial connections, with annual testing enforced through the utility's cross-connection control program. A property that recently added irrigation or changed its commercial water use should confirm whether a new backflow device and annual testing schedule are now required under current code.

Testing appointments are typically scheduled with some flexibility around a property's operating hours, and results are provided in writing the same day for the property owner's records and any required filing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is backflow testing legally required in Georgia?
Many municipal water authorities, including those serving Cobb County properties, require annual testing for properties with irrigation systems or certain commercial connections. Check with the specific water provider for the property in question.
What happens if a backflow device fails its test?
The device needs repair or replacement before it can pass, and most water authorities require documentation of the fix within a set timeframe.
How long does a backflow test take?
A standard test on a single device typically takes 30 to 45 minutes.
What happens if a property misses its annual backflow test deadline?
Water authorities can issue a notice of non-compliance and, in some cases, restrict service until testing is completed and documentation is filed.
Do residential homes without irrigation need backflow prevention?
Most standard residential connections without irrigation or special hazards don't require a dedicated backflow device beyond what's already built into standard fixtures.
Can a homeowner test their own backflow device?
No, testing must be performed by a certified tester using calibrated equipment, and the results typically need to be filed with the local water authority to count as valid.
