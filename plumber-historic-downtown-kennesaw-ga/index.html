Downtown Kennesaw grew up around the railroad as Big Shanty, and it is the part of the city where plumbing questions are most likely to be about age. This page covers the older streets and the small commercial buildings around them, and keeps what is documented separate from what only a look inside the building can confirm.

If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak In Historic Downtown Kennesaw

In an older building the main shutoff can be inside, in a crawl space or in a meter box that has been painted over, so find it before you need it.

  1. Close the main shutoff fully, at the meter or where the line enters the house.
  2. Keep anything electrical away from the wet area, and do not stand in standing water near outlets.
  3. Call (770) 214-4545 and tell dispatch your street and what you see.

What Is Documented About Downtown Kennesaw

The National Register of Historic Places lists several historic districts in Kennesaw, all entered on March 20, 1980: the Big Shanty Village Historic District (Park Avenue, Whitfield Place, and Main, Harris, Lewis and Cherokee Streets, with 36 contributing buildings and one contributing structure), the Cherokee Street Historic District, the North Main Street Historic District and the Summers Street Historic District. The Camp McDonald site was listed the same day, and the locomotive The General is listed separately, on display at the Southern Museum of Civil War and Locomotive History on Cherokee Street.

That tells us where the oldest part of the city is and that its streets are formally recognized. It does not tell us how any individual building is plumbed, and we will not suggest otherwise.

Older Buildings, Honest Expectations

As general plumbing information: houses and commercial buildings from the first half of the twentieth century commonly have been repiped or partly repiped over the decades, so a single building can contain several eras of pipe. Galvanized steel supply lines, cast iron drain stacks and clay or cast iron sewer laterals do appear in buildings of that age, but whether yours has them is something only inspection answers. A building near downtown is not automatically on old pipe, and a recent renovation can make a very old house plumbed mostly with modern material.

What older plumbing tends to show is gradual. Pressure that has slowly dropped, brown water after the line has been idle, a drain that slows in more than one fixture, or a cast iron stack that weeps at a joint. Our drain cleaning and sewer line repair pages explain what a camera inspection can tell you before any digging is discussed.

Small Storefronts And Mixed-Use Buildings

Around Main Street the buildings are a mix of homes and small businesses. A shop or restaurant has plumbing demands a house does not: floor drains, a mop sink, a grease-bearing kitchen line. We can help with the ordinary repairs on a small commercial unit, but anything involving a grease interceptor or a required inspection should be confirmed against the city's and the landlord's requirements first.

If Your Property Is In A Historic District

If you live or own inside one of the districts above, ask the City of Kennesaw whether exterior changes to a building need review before you start. Replacing a water heater or a pipe inside a wall is usually a different matter from altering a facade, but we would rather you confirm than assume. We will explain what a repair involves so you can ask the right question of the city.

What We Do Not Know

We have not verified the current pipe materials of any specific street, the sewer lateral material downtown, or search demand for this area name. Our homepage describes older homes around the historic corridor in general terms, and that is a general expectation, not a survey result.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Historic Downtown the same as Big Shanty?
Big Shanty is the older name of the town that became Kennesaw, and the Big Shanty Village Historic District is one of the National Register districts downtown.
Does an older building mean I need to repipe?
No. Many older buildings have already been repiped in part. Repiping is worth discussing only when an inspection and your symptoms point to it.
Do you work on small shops and restaurants downtown?
We handle ordinary repairs on small commercial units. Grease interceptors and inspected work should be checked against the city and your landlord first.
Do I need approval for plumbing work in a historic district?
Exterior changes may need review. Interior repairs usually do not, but ask the City of Kennesaw before starting.
